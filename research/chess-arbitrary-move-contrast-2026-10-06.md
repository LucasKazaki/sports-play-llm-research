# Comparing an arbitrary legal chess move

The Chess.com practice game asks why 20.Qc3 was worse than 20.Qe4. The earlier local paired review supplied one bounded engine comparison, but one game and one search cannot define a general move-quality rule. The new evaluator-only `scripts/chess_arbitrary_move_contrast_v1.py` accepts any selected legal move on a pinned standard-chess position. It searches without a root restriction to discover an alternative, then searches the selected move and that alternative together at 30,000 and 100,000 requested nodes. Scores are expressed from the mover's perspective, with mate and qualified bounds kept separate from exact centipawns.

An independent review caught an unsafe first version: it could combine final MultiPV ranks from different depths, or report a numeric loss when fake engine events said only one node had been searched. The revised source rejects unequal rank depths and requires each final score event to report at least four-fifths of its requested node budget. It also rejects Chess960 boards because the engine settings fix standard chess. Twenty-six fake-engine tests passed locally, and independent re-review passed this **bounded evaluator** scope. It retains no PV or post-move FEN in its output and has no generator interface.

These checks can produce a measured *pairwise observation* or an unresolved result. They do not establish why Stockfish prefers a move, whether a line is forced, or whether an explanation helps a learner. Source and engine binaries must be verified by the caller. A real Stockfish run on the pinned practice game and a separate no-forward model response remain next; neither has yet been established by these synthetic tests.
