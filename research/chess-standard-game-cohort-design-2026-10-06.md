# A small standard-game cohort for chess commentary

**Status, 6 October 2026:** design plus a tested offline parser for caller-pinned decompressed PGN bytes. No standard PGN was acquired, no game was selected, no engine or model was called, and no sealed holdout was opened for this note. The compressed-prefix broker and source binding are still pending. This is a development cohort proposal, not acceptance of the [chess commentary capability gate](chess-commentary-capability-gate-v1.md).

## Why use complete games

The current puzzle cohorts are useful for forcing moves, but they do not give us enough ordinary decisions: improving a piece, holding a weak square, simplifying into an ending, or explaining why a tempting move loses time or material. A small collection of played moves from complete standard games would let us exercise those explanations while keeping the game's legal history available to a *separate* verifier. The commentator would still see only the approved pre-move packet.

The proposed source is the **September 2025 standard rated-game PGN** at [Lichess's direct monthly URL](https://database.lichess.org/standard/lichess_db_standard_rated_2025-09.pgn.zst). Lichess lists standard rated games separately from variants and broadcasts, says each standard file covers one month, and provides a [download list](https://database.lichess.org/standard/list.txt) and [whole-file SHA256 list](https://database.lichess.org/standard/sha256sums.txt). Its [database page](https://database.lichess.org/) states that database exports are CC0 and may be used for research, modified, and redistributed. Broadcast exports have a different, attribution/share-alike license on that same page; this plan uses **only standard rated games**. The page says Zstandard archives can be partially decompressed, which makes a capped prefix practical. Its sample PGN includes a stable `Site` game URL, clocks, and sometimes `%eval` comments; Lichess says only about 6% of standard games contain those evaluations. We must treat the comments as source metadata, never as commentary input or authoritative labels. [Source: standard-game format and notes](https://database.lichess.org/).

September 2025 is a fixed, completed month. The [official checksum list](https://database.lichess.org/standard/sha256sums.txt) currently gives `b2b22f4c62c198191e370337249dcdbe3cff3f88e74f7c08b707daf6e0018f4d` for its **whole archive**. A prefix hash cannot validate that whole-file digest. The later acquisition receipt must retain the exact source-page, list/checksum snapshots, response status and headers, `Content-Range` or evidence that the server ignored Range, the received byte count, and SHA256 of the *retained prefix*. It must say `fullArchive: false`.

## Replay and exclusions

Read only complete PGN records produced before the cut-off; count and discard the final incomplete record. Require the standard initial position (`Variant` absent or `Standard`, no custom `SetUp`/`FEN`), an exact `https://lichess.org/<8-character-id>` Site URL, a parseable result, at least 16 plies, and error-free legal replay with a pinned `python-chess` version. A resignation or time loss need not end on a checkmated board. Record malformed headers, parser errors, illegal moves and truncated games separately. Derive each candidate's FEN **before** its played move; the replayed future is verifier-only data. Require a selected legal UCI move and compare its SAN to the replayed PGN. Exclude bullet games for this pilot by requiring the TimeControl initial seconds to be at least 300; keep the exact count excluded.

Use the namespace key `lichess:<Site game ID>` as the game identity. Build a trusted exclusion check from the frozen v1 game list and the existing v2 cohort, including its protected split, without displaying or reading sealed case payloads in this research task. The selector should receive only membership answers and exclusion counts from that verifier. If an approved sealed-safe membership check is unavailable, stop before selecting cases. Also exclude exact repeated game IDs, identical normalized UCI trajectories under another ID, and a candidate whose canonical pre-move position plus selected move matches an earlier accepted case or protected case. Retain hashes and counts of those checks. Only one position from any game may enter this cohort, so its development cases are game-disjoint from each other and from the existing puzzle cohorts. The Chess.com practice game remains a separate user-game example, not a development case.

## How to choose positions

This is a deliberately balanced **stress cohort**, not an estimate of how often these situations occur on Lichess. From all complete, replayable, non-excluded games in the prefix, rank games by SHA256 of the fixed string `standard-dev1|lichess:<id>`; inspect no more than the first 400 in that order. Consider played plies 16–160. Record every eligible candidate and its board-derived flags, then rank candidate positions by the same fixed-key hash of game ID and ply. Evaluate no more than 200 unique positions and select up to **eight per primary stratum** (40 total). If a game offers several candidates, at most one can be selected; scan the next ranked candidate when it is already used. Fix tie breaking lexically by game ID and ply. Publish both overlap flags and the single assigned stratum, with assignment priority `inferior → endgame → defensive → tactical → quiet`. Do not manually swap cases that read well.

The proposed strata are operational, rather than claims about human teaching quality:

| Stratum | Candidate rule and confirmation |
| --- | --- |
| Quiet | Played move is neither capture, check nor promotion, and the mover was not in check. The fixed-budget engine comparison finds no ≥150 cp loss. This includes positional moves; it does not certify their strategic explanation. |
| Defensive | The mover starts in check, or a queen/rook is directly attacked and the played move removes that immediate threat. Confirm legality and record whether a fixed-budget comparison finds the defense inferior. This permits examples of both sound and dubious defense. |
| Endgame | No queens remain and at most six non-pawn, non-king pieces remain before the move. A pawn ending or piece ending qualifies even if the move is quiet or defensive. |
| Tactical | The played move is a capture, check or promotion, or creates a directly checkable fork discovered by deterministic board replay. Call it a *forcing-move candidate* until an independent reviewer confirms the actual tactic; do not infer a forced combination from the PGN mark or a short engine search. |
| Inferior move | The selected played move loses at least 150 centipawns relative to the engine's best root move under the fixed search budget, from the mover's perspective. Do not mix mate scores, score bounds or missing searches into that numeric threshold; record them as separate unresolved or mate-transition categories. |

For the engine comparison, pin the project's Stockfish binary digest, version, options, threads and hash size. Search the unrestricted root and the played move constrained as a root move for **30,000 nodes each**, at most 400 such searches for the 200 candidate positions. Record both typed score values, perspective, cp/mate type, bounds, node counts and any failure. Do not call the played move *objectively bad* solely because of this shallow label; the number is a reproducible selection rule. For the 40 selected cases, repeat the two searches at **100,000 nodes each** as a stability check (at most 80 more searches). A label that crosses the threshold or changes score type is `unstable`, kept in the denominator and excluded from the confirmed inferior quota. There is no replacement after the 200-position cap. Engine principal variations, post-move FENs, PGN `%eval`, `!`/`?` marks, game result, clocks, later moves and these stratum labels stay in evaluator-only records. The generator receives only the capability gate's source-bound pre-move position, selected legal move, deterministic transition facts, permitted typed selected-move engine observation and declared vocabulary. The separate evaluator can inspect continuations **after** generation, never feeding them back into the generated text.

## What the receipt must count

The offline intake report must form an auditable waterfall: bytes requested and retained; decompressed bytes; complete and trailing partial PGNs; each rejected header/variant/time-control/ID/replay reason; exact-ID, trajectory and position-move duplicates; protected-cohort exclusions; games and candidate plies remaining; candidate counts and overlaps for all five strata; 400-game and 200-position cap losses; engine attempted/succeeded/failed/unstable by score type; and selected counts and shortfalls per stratum. Keep IDs and source hashes for selected **development** games, and a sealed-safe count-only exclusion receipt. A failed or empty intake is a result, not a reason to silently broaden the search. Never use these cases as a fresh holdout or quote a capability pass rate from them.
