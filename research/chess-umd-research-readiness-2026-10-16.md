UMD's data-management guidance treats a plan as an early research step covering data/code, responsibility, formats, documentation, storage, backup, sharing and preservation. Its FAIR guidance emphasizes descriptive metadata, persistent identifiers, trusted repositories, licenses and provenance. For this project, retain source revisions and licenses, exact cohorts/splits, environments, seeds, protocols, selection rules, raw predictions, full failure denominators and audited results. State which artifacts can be shared, which remain restricted and how to reproduce the permitted portion. Git history and hashes help; a documented preservation/sharing plan is still useful. These are project-specific applications of the guidance. [Data-management planning](https://researchdata.umd.edu/plan/data-management-planning-dmp), [FAIR data standards](https://researchdata.umd.edu/share-preserve/fair-data-standards)

UMD describes sensitive-data handling in terms of consent, de-identification and access controls, with service suitability dependent on classification. Any later recorded tutor interviews, learner interaction logs or student work would need a data-handling plan appropriate to that use. Do not infer approval for those records from the existing public-board workflow. [Handling sensitive data](https://researchdata.umd.edu/collect-manage/handling-sensitive-data)

This note makes no determination about a casual tutor demonstration, public chess-data analysis or any future study. [IRB review paths](https://research.umd.edu/resources/irb), [AI-specific IRB guidance](https://research.umd.edu/resources/irb-guidance)
