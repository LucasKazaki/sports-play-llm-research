# Archit working principles

These are project working rules from Archit's September 9, 2026 messages. They guide internal notes, prototype copy, and future coach-facing material.

1. Write in our own plain words first. If we cannot explain it simply, we do not understand it well enough yet.
2. Keep updates short: five or six points covering where we are, why it matters, what is missing, and the next honest step.
3. Do not overpromise. Separate a working system from a proven soccer or coaching result.
4. Use a machine-learning vision backbone to produce concrete evidence such as tracks, field location, time, and visible jersey information. A language model should search and explain that evidence, not guess a game from raw pixels.
5. Treat the current film setup walkthrough as a future discovery input. Do not claim knowledge of Maryland's workflow until Archit walks us through it.
6. Write for a general audience. Avoid AI jargon, filler, and polished-sounding claims without evidence.

## Current implementation meaning

The local prototype can search sealed reports and play source evidence. It cannot yet prove head-up decisions, off-ball movement quality, or player identity. Those features need a verified visual backbone, enough camera detail, and human review.
