## Gap against the local completed-game tool

The current [local tool guide](../prototype/CHESS_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md) and [page renderer](../scripts/chess_review_completed_game.py) describe an offline review of one selected move from a completed standard PGN. It shows before/after boards, deterministic capture/check facts, a separately searched alternative, and typed Stockfish observations. The renderer explicitly says **“Strategic explanation: Withheld”** and does not generate a teaching explanation or a move-quality label. Its two engine scores come from different searches, so it also avoids calling their difference a measured loss. The tool's current output therefore cannot yet reproduce the useful part of the Chess.com practice view: a concise, checkable answer to “why was this move worse, what could happen, and what was the better practical option?”

A suitable next comparison case is the visible 20.Qc3 versus 20.Qe4 contrast, using a **permitted, source-bound completed-game PGN** only if separately obtained. Freeze the pre-move board and the exact engine setting; have a no-forward generator state a bounded hypothesis without receiving continuation or answer labels; then let a separate evaluator replay the proposed capture/exchange line and check the material and threat claims. Record abstention if the source game or line cannot be verified. This is a proposed test, not a passing result. It does not relax the [no-forward commentary gate](chess-commentary-capability-gate-v1.md), and this one browser observation cannot support a general Chess.com parity claim or board-game expansion.

## A closer look at the reason, after the first practice page

The first page's pawn-capture example was incomplete as an explanation of the **difference** between the moves. A legal replay shows that `20.Qc3 Qxd4 21.Qxd4 Rxd4` loses White's d4 pawn in a queen exchange. But `20.Qe4 Qxd4 21.Qxd4 Rxd4` is legal too and reaches the **same board**. It would be wrong to say Qe4 simply prevents the pawn capture.

Qe4 also leaves White another option. In a separately saved, evaluator-only line after `20.Qe4 Qxd4`, `21.Qxb7+` takes a pawn with check and Black answers `21...Kd7`; Qxb7 is unavailable from c3. If Black effectively passed after Qe4, Qxb7 would be mate, while the v3 saved reply `20...Nd5` blocks the queen's route through d5. This is a concrete tactical question for the next teaching iteration, not proof of Stockfish's full reason or of a forced result. The one-call Stockfish 19 probe after Qe4 Qxd4 requested 100,000 nodes and five candidate replies. It placed Qxb7+ first with a **+78 centipawn upper bound** from White's perspective; that bound must not be presented as an exact advantage or compared numerically with the earlier 10,000-node root scores. The exact [probe](../artifacts/chess-user-reviews/chesscom-184866057876/qe4-qxd4-evaluator-probe-20261006.json) and [legal replay](../artifacts/chess-user-reviews/chesscom-184866057876/qe4-qxd4-legal-replay-20261006.json) are local, source-PGN-bound evidence. Neither was sent to a generator.

The GitHub mirror initially ran only 6 of 18 focused tests successfully because it excludes the local packet, engine binary, and demo PGN. A bounded test repair kept software controls self-contained and made those integration checks skip explicitly when their local assets are absent. The [portability receipt](../artifacts/chess-portability-20261006T0532Z/receipt.json) records **21 passed** in the original checkout and **16 passed, 5 skipped** in the clean sync worktree.
