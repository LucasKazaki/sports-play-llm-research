# Chess explanation teaching judgment: proposed human protocol

**Proposal, 6 October 2026. No blind cases or paired judgments have been run.** Freeze a numbered protocol, source list, sampling seed, reviewer identities and qualifications, prompts, model settings, capture rules, and analysis script before exposing any holdout output. The [no-forward capability gate](chess-commentary-capability-gate-v1.md) remains controlling. This note specifies how to test the user's *better than Game Review* teaching goal; it does not assert that goal has been met.

## What is being compared

Ask the same learner question for each move: **“What did this move change, why does that matter, and what should I look for next time?”** Compare the project's displayed explanation with the Chess.com Game Review explanation for that move, both captured before a judge sees either one. Freeze one exposure policy for all cases: the initial Coach message and one relevant Show/Best continuation if available, versus the project's initial lesson and one attached legal illustration if available. Preserve the original wording, move order, diagrams, click path, settings, date, and unavailable features; do not silently add an analyst's explanation to either side. Rate text-only explanations separately from any interactive lesson, because a static sentence cannot establish Retry-style teaching quality. Record word count and reading time rather than editing one system down to match the other.

This is an **outcome comparison**, not a claim that the systems received identical inputs. The project generator must obey its pre-move, no-forward field allowlist; Chess.com's review may use its own game analysis. Chess.com's [official feature description](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/8584089-how-does-game-review-work) documents Coach messages, classifications, predicted lines, Show options, and Retry feedback. Its [classification-change explanation](https://support.chess.com/en/articles/11845102-why-did-my-move-classification-change-in-game-review) says analysis strength can change a label. Therefore capture the exact review state and never treat its label or score as the answer key.

## Cases and blinding

- Use the [draft quality protocol's](chess-explanation-quality-protocol-draft-2026-10-06.md) proposed **200 primary cases from 200 distinct games**, 40 each for tactical choices, inferior moves, quiet/positional choices, defensive resources, and endgames. A case has one primary stratum, assigned from a separate development sample under frozen criteria; retain secondary tags. Include both sides to move and multiple source slices. Record rights and permitted use, complete candidate and exclusion counts, source bytes/hashes, legal replay, game and position deduplication, selection seed, and any review that could not lawfully or practically be captured. Do not replace a hard case after seeing an output. If 200 lawful paired Game Reviews cannot be preserved, report a smaller exploratory comparison and make no superiority claim.
- Exclude all project prompt-development, training, and known practice games, including the historical puzzles, the 24-position cohort, and the [open Chess.com game](chesscom-game-review-practice-2026-10-06.md). Its [four further observations](chesscom-review-breadth-observations-2026-10-06.md) are useful rubric probes, but all four are from that same game. General-model pretraining overlap may be unknown; disclose it.
- Generate each project answer once from its source-bound, audited pre-move packet. The generator may not see future play, a principal variation, alternative scores, solution/theme, Chess.com text or label, or evaluator-only evidence. After generation, a separate evaluator can replay named variations and compare properly typed engine observations. Freeze and retain the packet hash, raw request/response, model identity, latency, and error. Malformed, empty, timed-out, and interrupted attempts occupy their original case slots.
- Two independent judges, at least one with recorded chess teaching or playing qualifications, see the same pre-move board, selected move, learner question, and two anonymous explanations in randomized A/B order. They do not see product names, original Chess.com classifications, project prompt, or the other's scores. A separate accuracy dossier gives legal replays and qualified engine evidence without naming the product. Lock individual ratings before a third qualified adjudicator resolves material disagreement; retain every first rating and the adjudication reason. Have an independent evidence reviewer check the source, input audit, raw outputs, scoring and denominators.

## Two independent judgments per case

**1. Accuracy and restraint.** Split each explanation into atomic checkable claims. Mark each as replayed board fact, exact qualified engine observation, bounded strategic hypothesis, or abstention. Check legality, piece/square identity, conditional versus forced language, score perspective/type/bounds, and whether the stated reason survives the named reply. A factual error, illegal line, invented tactic, unsupported forced or winning claim, or asserted Stockfish *intent* is a critical error. A confident critical error gives that answer a teaching score of **0**, even if the rest sounds fluent. An offline rejection is a failed answer in the case denominator, not a clean abstention. If evidence cannot support a proposed causal claim, credit a precise partial abstention that still teaches verified board facts; penalize a full refusal on a clear case. Chess.com's output is checked by the same standard, not trusted as ground truth.

**2. Pedagogic usefulness.** Each judge scores 0–3 without using the engine number as a proxy: **0** misleading, failed, or no usable lesson; **1** legal facts, label, or score with no practical reason; **2** a relevant mechanism and choice or consequence that a learner can check, with uncertainty where needed; **3** a clear causal contrast, a plausible alternative or refutation, and a reusable cue for a similar position, without overclaiming. Record which of those elements was actually present. The judge then chooses **project / Game Review / tie** for “Which better helps this learner understand and avoid or repeat this move?” and gives one concrete reason. If both contain critical errors, record dual failure rather than awarding a teaching win. Keep the absolute scores, paired preference, critical-error flag, abstention type, and adjudication separate.

The [local model practice note](chesscom-no-forward-model-practice-2026-10-06.md) illustrates the difference: the played-move output was rejected, while the alternative-move output passed typed checks but repeated only its move and score. The [v7 note](chess-no-forward-teaching-v7-2026-10-06.md) describes a legally checked, narrow conditional lesson pattern and records no v7 model call at the time of writing. Neither is evidence of general teaching ability. The four observed Chess.com cards also expose different explanation types: missed development, material after exchanges, open-file play, and passed-pawn defense; reviewers should not infer those mechanisms merely from a review label.

## Proposed decision rule for a frozen successor

Use **all 200 scheduled cases** for rates and the paired comparison. Publish counts and 95% Wilson intervals overall and for each 40-case stratum. For displayed/admitted project answers require **100% correct asserted legal/board facts and qualified engine references**, zero contradicted categorical strategic claims, at least **95%** supported or expressly bounded nontrivial strategic claims, at least **170/200** adjudicated teaching scores of 2 or 3 and **32/40** in every stratum. Count every rejected, empty, timed-out, or missing output as 0. Report full and partial abstentions, p50/p95 latency, and all failure categories; the [draft protocol](chess-explanation-quality-protocol-draft-2026-10-06.md) also proposes no more than 10/200 full abstentions (4/40 per stratum) and a separately frozen 40-case insufficient-evidence control with at least 38/40 appropriate claim-level abstentions and zero invented facts. No percentage can hide a critical error in an admitted output.

For a **human-judged superiority** finding, additionally require at least **120 non-tied paired decisions**, at least **40 more project wins than Game Review wins** among all 200 cases, a two-sided 95% Wilson lower bound above 0.5 for the project win share among non-ties, and project wins at least equal to Game Review wins in **each** stratum. Failed outputs count as losses when the other answer is usable; dual failures and genuine equal lessons count as ties. Publish each judge's initial choices and adjudicated totals. These thresholds are proposed *before* a holdout exists and must be frozen, with any justified changes documented, before selection or scoring. Passing them supports only “qualified blind judges preferred these explanations on this sample.” It does not establish that every Stockfish move can be explained.

To say the product **actually teaches better**, follow the judged comparison with a separately preregistered, randomized learner study: blind learners to source, give one matched explanation, then score a new game-disjoint near-transfer choice and written reason against a source-independent chess rubric. Predeclare sample size, target learner level, outcome, exclusion rule, and uncertainty calculation before enrollment. Require a positive project-minus-Game-Review transfer effect whose 95% interval excludes zero, with no unresolved factual gate failure. Without that learner result, use the narrower judged-preference wording. Without a qualified chess judge, describe this as a limited local candidate. The chess no-forward gate and independent acceptance must pass before a professional-quality claim or any new board-game packet; human sports needs its own evidence and evaluation.
