# A conditional queen-defense comparison after the model proposes an alternative

The Chess.com practice position has a sharp but narrow lesson. After the shared reply `20...Qxd4`, `20.Qe4` makes `21.Qxb7+` possible. The bishop on f3 protects the queen on b7, and Black cannot take it with the king. After `20.Qc3 Qxd4`, `21.Qxc7+` also looks forcing, but c7 has no White defender and `...Kxc7` takes the queen. The [source-bound legal replay](chesscom-queen-capture-defense-2026-10-06.md) records those lines. The score probes do not establish that this difference caused Stockfish's root preference.

This v6 change is an **offline evaluator extension** of the v5 proposal. The generator input and its nine pre-move fields are byte-for-byte v5, and the model still has just two strict response shapes: one same-origin legal alternative or abstention. The later reply, checking captures, defenders, king moves, and bishop-removal counterfactual are never added to the generator packet. A new v6 result carries a hash of the full v5 evaluation and hashes of the v6, v5, and v4 evaluator source. Older responses and receipts are not reinterpreted as new model trials.

For a valid model proposal, the evaluator replays every reply that is legal in both branches and has the same SAN in both, as v4 does. A caller may supply an **evaluator-only focus reply**, bound to the exact source-packet hash. The focus never enters v5's generator messages. The evaluator still checks that the named reply is legal and has the named SAN in both branches. Without a focus, it searches all shared replies. This offline candidate cannot prove when the caller chose a focus; a future model capture must retain a pre-call focus selection if it wants to claim a predeclared scope. The result calls this a caller-supplied scope, not a predeclared one.

Within the stated reply scope, v6 considers only the moved queen's legal captures with check. The selected and proposed branches must capture the same kind of piece. The selected destination must have no friendly defender and admit an immediate legal king capture of the queen. The proposed destination must have exactly one friendly defender, a bishop, and must forbid that king capture. On a **copy** of the position, removing that bishop must leave a valid board and make the king capture legal. The evaluator enumerates *all* legal opponent replies after each capture; it says “only legal reply” only for a one-move list. If more than one pair meets the requirements **within the caller's scope**, it declines the stronger comparison instead of selecting a convenient pair. In an unscoped run, it may retain v5's generic legal illustration. In a focused run with no unique contrast, it abstains from teaching: v5's generic witness might refer to a different reply and must not masquerade as a lesson about the caller's focus.

The first native check exposed why scope matters. In the practice position, 36 replies have the same SAN in both branches; 28 separate reply/capture pairs meet the defense test. An unscoped evaluator must therefore decline the stronger sentence. The specifically researched `...Qxd4` branch has one qualified pair. A synthetic change that puts a White rook on c1 protects c7 after `...Qxd4`, so that branch has no qualified pair. The same changed position still has a different valid pair after `...Nc2`, which blocks the rook on the c-file. The code now records the reply scope in the result and the tests check both cases. This is an implementation finding, not a new model or teaching result.

The stronger wording is conditional on one common reply and the immediate king-capture test. It does not say that the proposed move is best, that the checking capture is generally safe, that `Qc3` forces a queen loss, or that this motif explains an engine score. Black has other replies, including `...Nd5` in the saved engine line. Both `Qc3 Qxd4 Qxd4 Rxd4` and `Qe4 Qxd4 Qxd4 Rxd4` reach the same board, so `Qe4` does not prevent the queen trade.

`tests/test_chess_no_forward_teaching_v6.py` is a synthetic software check using the practice FEN. It exercises the exact legal replies and defender lists, the queen-trade equality, a second defender, a defended selected destination, no bishop, a captured-piece mismatch, a nonchecking capture, a distant king, different-SAN and missing replies, real unscoped ambiguity, a reply focus with the wrong SAN or packet, an `...Nd5` focus that must not inherit v5's `...Qxd4` witness, and an unrelated `Qb3` proposal. The first native project-executor run passed 9 tests and failed two assumptions about unscoped uniqueness and the rook fixture; the failed receipt remains in the runtime. The repaired candidate now needs a materially changed native project-executor test and source review with complete bounded source bundles. Until those receipts exist, these are code and test candidates, not a verified practice outcome or commentary-quality result. Any later real model call needs a focus selected and recorded before generation, a fresh one-call capture, retained raw response, offline v6 evaluation, and an independent game-disjoint teaching check before broader claims.
