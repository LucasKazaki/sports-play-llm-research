# Comparing conditional-line coaching

The question is whether training descriptions of moves two and three helps the
local model describe its generated continuation accurately. The control is the
completed earlier 192-update adapter. The treatment is the terminal 192-update
adapter from the conditional-line experiment, fixed before its results are
known. Both are trained models. The original step-64 adapter and disposable
software-test weights are neither condition.

The two conditions use the same current-position inputs, inference code,
greedy decoding, seed, numerical settings and generation limits. A supplied
first move tests the model's response to that move; it is not an independently
predicted move. The shared inference wrapper keeps its original name in the
response envelope, while `trained_adapter` records the actual checkpoint.

The report separates several measurements:

- Raw and accepted move legality, plus agreement with the stored engine line.
- Recognized immediate capture and check facts.
- Recognized conditional capture and check facts for later plies, including
  false, unassessed, duplicate, missing and partial statements.
- Complete joint criteria and descriptive paired counts at the source-game
  level. Three presentations of one game are not three independent games.

The conditional scorer audits an exact grammar. Other prose, strategic
reasons, instructional usefulness and playing strength remain unmeasured.
Legal alternatives may disagree with the teacher line while describing true
board changes. Increased template coverage and lower loss do not establish
broader chess understanding. Losses from different target definitions are
not compared as evidence of improvement.
